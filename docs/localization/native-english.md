# JUYU English content review

English articles are separate drafts. Write them for an English-speaking teammate; do not paste a sentence-by-sentence translation of the Chinese source. Each language goes through its own review and publication. An unpublished English draft must never appear in employee search or language switching.

## Voice

- Lead with the action or answer. Use short, direct sentences and the same terms throughout one procedure.
- Name the person who acts: “Ask the account owner to…” is clearer than “The relevant party shall…”.
- Use natural interface language: “Save article”, “Try again”, “Back to top”, “Contact support”. Avoid formal constructions such as “proceed to carry out” or “kindly perform”.
- Explain JUYU-specific workflows the first time they appear. Keep product names, role names, and button labels exact when they refer to the actual interface.
- Rewrite examples for the reader’s context. Do not invent policy, eligibility, deadlines, fees, or security steps that are absent from the approved Chinese source.

## Review before publishing

1. Check the business meaning against the current approved Chinese version and flag any policy difference for a subject-matter reviewer.
2. Read the English article aloud. Remove literal phrasing, unnecessary passive voice, and steps that require the reader to guess who should act.
3. Check every heading, button name, link target, image caption, table label, warning, and error state in English.
4. Check that account, role, domain, and approval terms are consistent across the article and the UI.
5. Have a fluent English reviewer approve the wording separately from the normal content and permission review. Automated checks cannot certify native-quality writing.

If no reviewed English version exists, keep the Chinese article available and show that English is not yet published. Never use machine-generated placeholder text as a published English article.
